Points of Unity

This is a draft working document, not a final word. It is meant to serve as a common "floor" from which we all agree to build the needed details and systems to bring Mutual Aid to life in the Triangle.

More detailed topics are often explored on our Patreon site in our meetings and working groups.

1. Housing, Food, and Care Are Non-Negotiable

We believe survival needs—shelter, nourishment, healthcare, and connection—are not privileges to be earned, but rights to be honored. We reject systems that tie basic human needs to employment, citizenship, paperwork, sobriety, compliance, or worthiness narratives.

2. Solidarity Over Charity

We act with, not for. Our work is mutual and reciprocal, not transactional. We don't serve, we share. We trust that everyone has something to give and something they need. Our organizing rejects saviorism and centers dignity, autonomy, and collective liberation.

3. No One is Disposable

We organize from a deep belief that all people are worthy of care, regardless of background, past harm, ability, documentation status, mental health, housing situation, or other factors. We meet people where they are, not where the system says they should be.

4. Disasters Don't Break the System; They Reveal It.

Storms, floods, pandemics, and displacement don’t create injustice, they expose it. We don’t wait for the state to solve what it helped cause. We respond from the ground up, guided by care, community knowledge, relationships, and resistance.

5. Decentralization Is a Strength

We trust horizontal, consent-based structures. Leadership is shared, emergent, and accountable. We build trust through transparency, aligned values, and action, not titles. Our strength is in networks, not bosses.

6. Our Safety Is Interconnected

Our safety is something we build together. It doesn’t come from control, but from reciprocal care. Our safety is intertwined with yours. We have a duty to get through this together. We protect each other not by closing off, but by opening up—with boundaries, with trust, and with the kind of expanded generosity and mutualism that makes all of us more secure.

7. Grief, Rage, and Joy Belong Here

Mutual aid is not just logistics; it's human. It's emotional labor, spiritual work, and political defiance. Sometimes it’s spreadsheets and trash bags. Sometimes it’s crying in the kitchen. It’s all part of it. We make space for the full range of human feeling in our work.

8. We Organize to End the Conditions Charity Responds to—and Depends on

We respond to emergencies, but we are not here to make injustice more comfortable. Our goal is to uproot the systems that create the emergencies. We fight for a future where mutual care is not an exception, but the norm.

9. Liberation is Local, Collective, and In Motion

Our work begins in the Triangle, with the neighbors, neighborhoods, and land we share. But our vision is global. We show up how we can, where we are, connecting neighbor to neighbor, block to block, and struggle to struggle.
